# Problem Definition Sheet (hand-in #1)

**Problem in one line.** When many drivers report delays at once, SetuHaul must answer each one quickly, in their language, with a dock slot that is *actually* feasible, and must never promise the same capacity twice.

**Users.** Drivers (WhatsApp/SMS, English, Hindi, Hinglish); warehouse coordinators and shift managers (approve, take over); head of operations (policy, risk).

**Scale to design for** (brief, p.1): 280–360 loads/day, 6 facilities, 32 docks, 190–240 appointments/day, 15–25 exception messages on a normal day, **20–35 messages in 30 minutes** during a disruption, 5 coordinators per shift, **no GPS**.

**Success measures** (brief, p.9) and where we report them: `scenarios/scenario_metrics.json`, `evals/report.md`.

## What the snapshot cannot tell you

Live truck position (no GPS); whether a driver's ETA is honest; real dock productivity; labour availability per dock; customer cut-off times; what coordinators actually decided outside the system (phone calls).

## Data issues (20, each proved by a query)

Full list with queries and results: `audit/findings.md` (generated by `python -m audit.run_audit`). Highlights:

- **A01/A07** Active bookings on docks too weak for the load (SHP1005 20.5 t on D3 20 t; SHP1014 21 t pending on D1).
- **A02** D3 breakdown starts 09:15 but slot 09:00–10:00 stays OPEN with a confirmed truck.
- **A03** Seven bookings whose unload is longer than their slot.
- **A05/A06** One driver on two in-transit trucks; one vehicle on several live shipments.
- **A08** No single as-of time in the snapshot.
- **A09** No uniqueness on message ids or exception dedupe keys; a retry arrived with a new id.
- **A11/A12** Views overstate ETA confidence and cannot answer "available for this truck".
- **A13/A14** Escalation contact vacant with no email; a FAILED email sits next to an "escalated" thread.
- **A20** What the two unique indexes do **not** cover: interval overlap across slots, dock compatibility, dock events, cancelled shipments, holds.
