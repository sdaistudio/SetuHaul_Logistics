# Decision register (hand-in #2)

Every decision the brief lists, with the answer, the reason and the evidence. Code references point into `setuhaul/`.

## Understand

| # | Decision | Answer | Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | What must we collect before showing options? | Only two things the backend cannot know: **which shipment** (only if the driver has more than one live trip and the evidence cannot narrow it) and **a clock-time ETA** (exact, approximate or a range). Everything else (dock type, weight, priority, unload time, current booking, rules) is looked up, never asked. Optional constraints ("after 12", "must leave by 1:30") are used if offered, never requested. | Asking for a fact the backend holds wastes the driver's time; asking nothing produces wrong options. | `scenarios/transcript.md`: S-D (two trips, one question), S-G (vague "thoda late", one question), S-A (ETA + constraint in one message, zero questions). 3 clarification questions across 12 cases. |
| D2 | How does a conversation link to driver, shipment, appointment? | Phone → `drivers.phone` → driver. Shipment: explicit reference (SHP id, order number or its last 3 digits) > the thread's existing shipment > the only live trip > the only *in-transit* trip for a delay / the only *at-gate* trip for an early-arrival message > ask. Appointment = the shipment's `is_current` active appointment. | Deterministic narrowing with evidence, never a guess between equals. | S-F: DRV007 is on two in-transit trucks (audit A05) so the agent asks; "016 wala" resolves by order suffix. `workflow._resolve_shipment`. |
| D3 | How do we set the revised ETA and say how sure we are? | Gate check-in beats everything (fact). Otherwise newest ETA record wins. Confidence becomes a *planning ETA*: HIGH = point, MEDIUM = +15 min, LOW = upper edge of the window (or +30). "Late by X" is anchored to the original plan and the assumption is stated back to the driver. | Plan for the late edge so we do not promise a slot the truck will probably miss; say the assumption so the driver can correct it without an extra question. | ETA rule applied to two messages: SHP1013 "late by one hour" → 11:00, planned 11:15 (S-C); SHP1017 "between 12:30 and 13:00" → planning 13:00 (eval F11). `eta.py`. |

## Decide

| # | Decision | Answer | Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D4 | What makes a slot feasible for this shipment? | Rules R1–R8 in `feasibility.py`: active shipment; dock type (reefer → refrigerated dock, > 25 t → heavy dock, standard → standard only); load ≤ dock limit; slot OPEN; start inside the window after ETA + 15 min gate-to-dock; no overlap with in-progress overruns, other bookings (at *their* trucks' expected start), live holds or dock events; facility hours (start after 21:00 → approval); driver deadlines. One booking per slot. | Many rules apply at the same time; each failure has a reason code a person can read. | Written check on three shipments: SHP1016 heavy → D6 only from 12:40 (F02); SHP1014 21 t → never D1/D3 (F03, flags its own pending D1 request, audit A07); SHP1015 reefer → none (F01). 11/11 feasibility cases pass. |
| D5 | What does "available" mean while another driver is deciding? | **Shown ≠ reserved.** An option is available to everyone until somebody commits it. Shown options are re-checked at commit; if gone, the driver is told plainly and gets fresh options in the same reply. A short **hold** (5 min) exists only between "driver picked" and "commit finished / coordinator decides". | Reserving on display would let one slow driver block scarce capacity during a spike; re-checking keeps promises true. | S-B: DRV012 was shown D4 12:30, DRV006 committed it first, DRV012 sees "taken by another truck" (1 of 23 shown options went stale). State needed: `slot_holds` (+ unique partial indexes), `shown_at` per thread. |

## Commit

| # | Decision | Answer | Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D6 | When does an option become a hold, request, reservation, confirmed booking? | SHOWN (nothing reserved) → driver picks → HELD (row in `slot_holds`, 5-min TTL, unique per slot) → if any approval trigger fires: PENDING_CONFIRMATION (appointment occupies the slot; coordinator has 20 min) else CONFIRMED (written in the system of record, old appointment cancelled in the same transaction). Code moves every state; the coordinator moves PENDING. The driver sees "Nothing is booked until you choose" / "NOT confirmed yet" / "Booking confirmed". | Showing is not reserving; reserving is not confirming. | `capacity.py`; tests `test_hold_blocks_then_expires`, `test_pending_confirmation_times_out`; S-F pending → approved at 09:56. |
| D7 | How do we order simultaneous requests when capacity is short? | Options are ranked by a **deterministic branch-and-bound look-ahead** over every truck still needing a dock (`solver.py`, costs in `rules/rules.yaml`): a slot that would strand another truck is ranked down (S-A/S-B: DRV006 steered off the only slot DRV012 can use). At commit: **first valid commit wins**, serialised per facility (BEGIN IMMEDIATE / SQS FIFO + Postgres advisory lock), always re-checked. (With `option_ranking: earliest` in rules.yaml the fallback is earliest start, no-approval first.) Priority comes only from `shipments.priority_code`; claims in text ("urgent", "VIP") are logged and ignored. Facility-wide fairness is the optimal re-plan's job: moving a promise that still works costs `move_kept_promise` and is only ever proposed to a coordinator. | Chat-level FCFS is simple and predictable for drivers; facility-level priority needs the whole picture and breaks promises, so a person approves it. | Policy test on competing trucks (`scenarios/scheduler_runs.json`, 09:30): the proven-optimal plan gives the critical load 0 min waiting vs 40 under FCFS, 6 changes to promises vs 9, and 3,625 priority-weighted waiting minutes vs 5,825. Priority-claim test passes. |
| D8 | (Optional) When does the facility schedule recalculate, what is fixed, what is the objective? | Triggers: ETA update, gate check-in, dock event, every 15 min. Fixed: trucks on a dock, dock events (a closed dock is closed). Objective = the cost function in `rules/rules.yaml` (priority-weighted waiting, lateness vs promise, moving a promise, approval, no dock). Solved **exactly** by branch and bound with an admissible bound, anytime with a proven gap; cross-checked against OR-Tools CP-SAT. Engine ladder: B&B → CP-SAT → OptaPlanner/Timefold as facilities grow (`docs/03_solver_design.md`). | 8/8 instances identical to CP-SAT (`evals/report.md`); full 16-truck re-plan proven optimal in 0.1–1.1 s. | Replay before/after the spike and after SHP1007 check-in (S-run 09:30 and 10:00). |
| D9b | When does a booking stop blocking capacity? | When even the optimistic ETA cannot reach its window (certain miss). The commit that takes the slot cancels the dead booking in the same transaction and notifies dispatch; the truck is re-planned. | Same rule in feasibility, solver and commit, so options never contradict the plan. |
| D9 | Stale options, cancellations, duplicates, retries? | Stale: re-check at commit + `shown_at`. Cancellation: frees the slot immediately; cancelled shipment → "contact dispatch". Duplicates: exact external id **or** same driver + same normalised text (≥ 4 words) within 10 min; short answers like "1" are never content-deduped. Retries: idempotency key per commit; SQS FIFO dedupe. | Chat channels repeat messages (THR001/THR009 retry has a *new* message id). | S-A 09:35 retry suppressed; `test_duplicate_retry_is_suppressed`; `test_idempotent_commit`; S-I cancelled shipment. |

## Fail safely

| # | Decision | Answer | Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D10 | No feasible slot? | Do not invent one. Escalate with a complete record (facts, ETA, every option tried and why it failed, recommendation), route to a contact with a working channel, tell the driver not to go to the dock until contacted. | An invented answer is worse than an escalation. | S-K reefer SHP1015 and S-B DRV012 deadline: 2/2 hand-off records complete. |
| D11 | What do we say when the preferred slot is refused? | Say what happened and what is possible, in the driver's language, without blame: "Sorry, dock D4 at 12:30 was just taken by another truck" + fresh options; "Arriving early does not move you ahead of trucks already scheduled…". | Refusals without a reason cost trust. | Two refusal messages in `scenarios/transcript.md` (S-B, S-EARLY). |
| D12b | Is a person's approval re-checked? | Yes. `decide_pending` re-runs feasibility; an infeasible approval is refused with reasons. | Scenario 09:49: coordinator's approval of APT1014A (21 t on a 20 t dock) refused. |
| D12 | Which decisions need human approval or takeover? | Safety incident (immediate, shift manager); no feasible slot; driver asks for a person; start after 21:00; low-confidence ETA booking; contradictory data (driver on two trucks, vehicle shared); clarification limit (2); guardrail block; any swap that breaks a promise; penalties/compensation/disputes. | Safety, penalties and disputes carry risk. | `escalation.TRIGGERS`; S-J safety, S-F data conflict, S-C pending. |
