FDE ACADEMY | MINI PROJECT 2 | SETUHAUL LOGISTICS

Deliverable 6

Client Readout

Driver delays at Jaipur DC: what we found in your data, what we built, what it showed on 4 August, and what we need you to decide

A plain-language summary for SetuHaul's head of operations and warehouse planner. No technical background is needed. Every number comes from your own snapshot of 4 August 2026, replayed through the working system.

Client: SetuHaul Logistics Pvt. Ltd. (FDE Academy challenge)

Snapshot: 4 August 2026, Jaipur DC and Gurugram Cross-Dock (Asia/Kolkata)

Version: 1.0 | 18 November 2026

Audience: SetuHaul head of operations; warehouse planner, Jaipur DC

1. Where you are today

On 4 August, Jaipur DC expected 20 trucks at its 6 dock doors. Ten drivers opened twelve WhatsApp threads about delays, early arrivals or questions. One door broke down at 09:15. A coordinator was handling all of this by phone, e-mail and chat, while trying to keep a plan that changed every few minutes.

That is a normal day, and the people on it did a lot right. But the data shows where the current way of working lets you down.

2. What we found in your data

  • A booking that could never work, found too late. The reefer load of dairy for FreshBasket (ORD-260804-015) was booked on 1 August for 17:00 on Door D5. Two hours later, maintenance was planned on D5 from 18:00. Nobody saw the clash for three days. On the 4th, the warning e-mail went to a night-shift address that bounces, at 16:25.

  • A promise made twice. Two confirmed bookings sat on the same slot for three days. Your system now shows only one of them, because it overwrites history.

  • A driver on two moving trucks. DRV007 is recorded as driving two trucks at once. One of those ETAs cannot be true, and the plan relies on both.

  • Bookings that break your own rules. Some loads are booked on doors that cannot take their weight, and slots on a broken door still show as open.

  • Drivers left without an answer. Three drivers got no reply at all, and one request sat unsent.

The data audit (deliverable 1) lists 36 findings like these, each with the query that proves it.

3. Why this matters

  • Detention and missed pickups. A driver who waits at your gate is not earning, and carriers charge for it. One driver that morning had to leave by 13:30 for his next pickup; a simple first-come-first-served plan would have left him no door that let him make it.

  • Cold chain. A reefer truck that cannot unload must park with its fridge running, and its dairy load and e-way bill both have a clock on them.

  • Customer promises. A CRITICAL load (CareSupply Rajasthan) was competing for the same doors as everything else.

  • Your coordinator's time. Most of the morning's messages needed the same lookups: where is the truck, which door fits, what is free. That is work a system can do, so people can spend their time on the decisions that really need them.

4. What we built

A WhatsApp assistant for drivers that works in English, Hinglish and Hindi, backed by a booking engine that cannot double-book.

  • It reads the message and looks up the rest. It asks the driver only for what it cannot know, usually nothing and at most one question.

  • It offers only slots that really work for that truck: the right door type and weight, time for the unload, the driver's own deadline, and no clash with trucks already at the doors.

  • Nothing is booked until the driver chooses, and the choice is checked again at that moment. If someone else took the slot a second earlier, the driver is told why and gets fresh options in the same reply.

  • Your priorities come from your records, not from who shouts loudest. HIGH and CRITICAL loads get first claim on scarce slots; a confirmed booking is only moved by a coordinator, and never in a way that breaks another driver's deadline.

  • It hands over the hard cases (accidents, no workable slot, data that does not add up) to the right person, by a channel that works, with everything they need on one screen.

  • It warns early. Every time something changes (an ETA, a breakdown, a cancellation), it re-checks every booking and tells people before the truck arrives at a closed door.

IN PLAIN WORDS | One way to picture it

It is like a good railway reservation clerk who knows every train, never sells the same berth twice, speaks the passenger's language, and calls the station master the moment something is beyond the counter's authority.

We also tried to break it on purpose: ten drivers grabbing the same slot at the same instant, fifty times in a row. Each time exactly one got it, and the other nine got a polite "just taken" with fresh options. When we switched our protections off, all ten "got" the slot. That is the proof that the protections, not luck, are doing the work (deliverable 5).

6. The policies we applied, which need your sign-off

These are business choices, not technical ones. We made a sensible choice for each so the system could run; you may change any of them, and the system follows.

Policy What we assumed Who should decide
What a slot means A start window: the truck can start any time inside it and must reach the gate within 30 minutes of its start. Unloads may run past the slot's end if the door is free. Warehouse planner
Time from gate to door, and between trucks 15 minutes gate to door; 10 minutes changeover Warehouse planner
How sure we must be of an ETA We plan on the late side: no buffer for a clock time, 15 minutes for "around", 30 for vague reports Head of operations
Who gets scarce slots Priority from the shipment record; slots for HIGH and CRITICAL loads, and for trucks with a tight deadline, protected for 20 minutes; otherwise the first valid choice wins Head of operations
Moving a confirmed booking Only by a coordinator, only if the gain is real (30 minutes or more), never breaking anyone's deadline Head of operations
Evening unloads Any start at 21:00 or later needs a coordinator's approval Warehouse planner
Standard loads on the heavy or reefer door Not allowed unless a coordinator overrides Warehouse planner

7. What it will not do

  • It will not decide anything about driver safety, penalties, compensation, disputes, hazardous or regulated loads. These always go to a person.

  • It will not invent a slot when none exists. It says so and hands over.

  • It will not let a message's word "urgent" jump the queue. Priority comes from your records.

  • It will not trust its own reading of a message over your data. If an ETA does not add up (a truck that is still in your yard cannot be on the road), it asks a coordinator.

8. Risks, and how we handle them

Risk How we handle it
Drivers do not reply, or reply in ways we did not expect One clarifying question at most; after two unanswered questions a person takes over
A wrong reading of a message Every booking is checked against your data before it is made; the reply states what we understood so the driver can correct it
Your data is wrong (like the driver on two trucks) We lower our confidence, flag it to the coordinator, and do not build on it
Coordinators get too many hand-offs We measure it from day one; the pilot only goes live if hand-offs stay manageable
WhatsApp or a mailbox is down Hand-offs skip broken channels (the bouncing night-shift address is never used) and fall back to phone

  2. Confirm what a slot means at Jaipur: a start window, or a fixed start with 30 minutes' grace. This one decision changes who counts as a no-show.

  3. Fix the night-shift mailbox and tell us who is the coordinator after 18:00.

  4. Tell us who is driving the second truck recorded against DRV007.
