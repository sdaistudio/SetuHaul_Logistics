1. The problem in one paragraph

When a wave of delays hits, 20-35 drivers message SetuHaul within half an hour, while the number of usable replacement dock slots stays small. Each driver needs a quick, clear answer in their own language: either a slot their truck can actually make, or an honest "a person will call you". Underneath, no slot may ever be promised twice, scarce capacity must go by business priority and existing promises rather than typing speed, and every hard rule must hold. The hard rules: dock type, weight, breakdowns, operating hours and the driver's own deadline.

DECISION | How might we...

...give drivers a conversational way to report delays, ask questions and consider revised appointments, while many simultaneous requests meet limited warehouse capacity, without infeasible or conflicting promises? (The brief's problem statement. We keep it unchanged and measure against it.)

FDE NOTE | The number that drives the design

Computing load is not the constraint: one small server handles a message a minute easily. Coordinator attention is. That is why the design aims to resolve about 80% of cases without a person (we measured 10 of 13), and sends a person only the decisions rules cannot make, each with the options already computed.

IN PLAIN WORDS | In plain words

The hard part is not the computer keeping up. It is making sure five busy people at the warehouse are only phoned about the few cases that really need a human, like a hospital triage desk that handles routine bookings itself and pages a doctor only for emergencies.

4.4 Unknowns (the snapshot cannot tell us)

  • Where trucks really are. There is no GPS, and two ETAs are physically impossible (F11, F13).

  • What "now" is. Rows run from 07:10 to 16:30 (F15). We solve this with an explicit as_of time and a replay that rewinds the snapshot to 08:20.

  • Real dock productivity and labour. Whether a free door has a crew and a forklift (Q12).

  • Outbound use of the same doors (Q11).

  • Commercial terms. Detention free time and rates, customer cut-offs, penalties (Q25, Q26).

  • Decisions made by phone outside the system. For example, who approved APT1014A's pending request.

  • Tomorrow's slots. None are published (F23).

5. What the data tells us about the current process

The audit is not just a list of bad rows. Read together, the findings describe how the current process fails:

  1. Nobody re-checks a promise after it is made. The reefer booking was made on 1 August at 12:00. Maintenance on its only possible door was planned two hours later. Nothing flagged the clash for three days (F07). Breakdowns leave slots marked OPEN (F02), and ETA updates leave dead bookings holding capacity (F06).

  2. The chat path books without checking. The current chat flow created a pending booking of a 21 t load on a 20 t door (F30).

  3. History is overwritten. Two confirmed bookings shared one slot for three days, and the table can no longer show it (F08).

  4. Plans ignore that a truck can only be in one place. Three trucks are scheduled on a second trip while still on their first (F12, F14); one "departed" while still inside Jaipur DC (F13); one driver drives two trucks (F11).

  5. Messages fall through. Three drivers got no reply (F27), a retry created a duplicate thread (F25, F26), a pending request was never sent (F28), and the escalation e-mail went to an address nobody owns (F29).

CHANGE FROM THE EXISTING DESIGNS | What each pattern changed in our design

1 became the booking health monitor (re-check on every event). 2 became one booking service that is the only writer. 3 became appointment_events, an append-only history. 4 became plausibility-checked ETAs. 5 became the outbox, dedupe, reply-time alarm and channel-aware routing.

Severity: Critical | Area: Capacity | Also found by: Doc A A01, Doc A A07, Doc B D1, Doc B D2

Why it matters. Two promises that cannot be kept: the truck would be refused at the dock.

Design response. Feasibility rule F2 (weight) on every option and again inside the commit transaction; re-plan both trucks.

F02. Dock events are not reflected in slot status

Severity: Critical | Area: Capacity | Also found by: Doc A A02, Doc B D5

Why it matters. A slot on a broken dock still looks OPEN, so a naive availability check would offer it.

Design response. Derive slot usability from dock_status_events at the as-of time; never trust slot_status alone.

Proof query

JOIN docks d ON d.dock_id=e.dock_id

WHERE sl.slot_status='OPEN' AND sl.slot_start_ts < COALESCE(e.event_end_ts,'9999') AND sl.slot_end_ts > e.event_start_ts

F04. Planned unload is longer than the booked slot

Severity: High | Area: Capacity | Also found by: Doc A A03, Doc B D3

Why it matters. Each of these spills into the next window on the same dock; delays cascade.

Design response. Model dock occupancy as time intervals (start + unload + changeover), not as one slot per truck.

Proof query

AND s.expected_unload_min > ROUND((julianday(sl.slot_end_ts)-julianday(sl.slot_start_ts))*1440)

F05. Booked work already collides on the same dock (cascade proof)

Severity: High | Area: Capacity | Also found by: New (in neither existing design)

Why it matters. The plan itself is infeasible before any driver is late: the next truck cannot start on time.

Design response. Interval check (no overlap per dock) in the feasibility layer and a DB trigger as the last guard.

Proof query

WITH b AS (

SELECT a.appointment_id, sl.dock_id, d.dock_code, sl.slot_start_ts AS st,

datetime(sl.slot_start_ts, '+'||s.expected_unload_min||' minutes') AS planned_end_utc

FROM appointments a JOIN shipments s USING(shipment_id)

JOIN appointment_slots sl ON sl.slot_id=a.slot_id JOIN docks d ON d.dock_id=sl.dock_id

WHERE a.appointment_status IN ('PENDING_CONFIRMATION','CONFIRMED','IN_PROGRESS'))

SELECT x.dock_code, x.appointment_id AS first_booking, x.st AS first_start,

y.appointment_id AS next_booking, y.st AS next_start,

CAST(ROUND((julianday(x.planned_end_utc)-julianday(y.st))*1440) AS INT) AS overlap_min

FROM b x JOIN b y ON x.dock_id=y.dock_id AND y.st > x.st

WHERE julianday(x.planned_end_utc) > julianday(y.st)

AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM b z WHERE z.dock_id=x.dock_id AND z.st > x.st AND z.st < y.st)

Result (2 rows)

dock code first booking first start next booking next start overlap min
D1 APT1014A 11:00 APT1017 12:00 15
D2 APT1002 08:00 APT1004 09:00 10
